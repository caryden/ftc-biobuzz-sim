Match Lab

Set up your alliance and see the scores that it is likely to post, and how often it beats an opponent. We measured every effect below in a physics simulator by changing one thing at a time. The estimate stacks those measurements, so read it as a guide, not as a promise.

Scores are treated as normal distributions, and the two alliances as independent. Your spread is 7 points plus 7% of your mean, fitted to the match data. Gains shrink as they stack: the factor is fitted to one combined build, which scored 508 where the sum of its parts says 538. A launcher that is tighter than the reference isn't measured, so it counts as the reference.

What each choice is worth #

Points per alliance against the baseline robot, with one standard error on each side. The shaded band is 25 points: inside it, 12 matches can't tell a change from luck.

Every match, one dot #

Each dot is one match of the red alliance with that change, in bins of 20 points. The tick is the mean, and the line is the baseline mean. A wide row is a robot whose results you can't count on.

Where the points come from #

A robot's 120 s of TELEOP

Driving is 73% of the match and launching is 5%. A shorter trip buys more than a faster shooter.

Better decisions, the same robot

Combined score of both alliances on 24 fixed seeds, after each change that was kept. The last step is the lighter, smaller robot.

When one robot defends

Points that the defender gives up against points that it takes from the opponent. A weak alliance still lost all 24 matches with a defender. The mixed alliance, a strong robot with a weak partner, went from 0 wins to 15 of 24. The only contact rule in the simulator is the PIN rule, G421, and the opponents here don't adapt to a defender.

Your score doesn't depend on theirs

Without defense, an alliance scores the same alone, against itself, and against a stronger opponent. That is why the lab models each alliance from its own robot.

The baseline robot is , and geared for 600 rpm at the wheel, with a rear-facing shooter and a 95% reliable intake. The meta alliance is two robots with a four-element catapult, , 500 rpm, and intakes at both ends.

The simulator uses the listed ball weights but estimates how the balls bounce and roll. It plays no defense unless you choose it, and doesn't check whether a design can be built. The written lessons are in docs/top-ten.md, and the data is in experiments/ of the repository.
